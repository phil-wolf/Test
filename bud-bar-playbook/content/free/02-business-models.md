# Business models

## Three common business models

Your business model decides what you charge for and what you handle at the event.

1. **Service-fee / BYO (bring your own):** The host or guests supply their own cannabis. You charge for staff, setup, accessories, and the guest experience. You do not sell cannabis.
2. **Licensed partner:** You work with a licensed cannabis business, such as a dispensary or delivery service, that handles any product sales. You provide the event service.
3. **Licensed operator:** Your company holds a cannabis license that covers event service, in states that offer one. [PHILIP TO VERIFY]

## Which model is allowed depends on the state

Each state sets its own rules, and they change. Before you pick a model, find out:

- Whether cannabis use at a private event is allowed in your state [PHILIP TO VERIFY]
- Whether a service company may handle or serve cannabis the host provides [PHILIP TO VERIFY]
- Whether "gifting" setups (cannabis "included" with a paid service) are allowed [PHILIP TO VERIFY]
- Whether your city or county has stricter rules than the state
- Whether the venue allows it at all

> Do not take a deposit until you have confirmed your model is allowed in your state, county, and city. [PHILIP TO VERIFY]

## Why many new bud bars start with service-fee / BYO

- **Lowest startup cost:** no product inventory to buy
- **You control your revenue:** you are paid for service, not product margin
- **Easier to explain:** venues and planners understand "we provide the staff and setup"
- **Still not automatic:** you must still confirm the model is allowed where you work [PHILIP TO VERIFY]
